FoundationMethod

Scorecards vs rubrics

A scorecard lists what you measure. A rubric defines what each score level looks like with behavioral anchors. Most teams skip the rubric — that's where score drift starts.

ToolQuestion it answersOwner
ScorecardWhich 5–7 competencies matter for this role family?Hiring manager + HRBP
RubricWhat does a 3 vs 4 look like for each competency?HRBP (with manager review)
Interview kitWhich question targets which competency?HRBP
Debrief protocolHow do we combine scores into a decision?Recruiter facilitates
RuleAssign each competency to one interviewer. When everyone owns everything, nothing is scored rigorously and "culture fit" wins.

What this guide assumes

  • You have a kickoff worksheet (Beginner p.12) for the role.
  • You can name the role family — IC sales, HR generalist, ops, engineering manager — before opening AI.
  • You have an ATS or a tracked spreadsheet for evidence storage.

FoundationKnockouts

Minimum bar & role family

Before interviews, document the minimum total score (e.g., 16/28) and knockouts (e.g., must score 3+ on Communication). Knockouts protect against weighted averages hiding a single fatal gap.

Competency models by role family

Role familyCore competencies (5–7)Common knockouts
IC salesDiscovery, objection handling, pipeline discipline, communication, coachabilityPipeline discipline < 3
HR generalistJudgment, stakeholder communication, process orientation, ownership, written clarityStakeholder comms < 3
OperationsSystems thinking, prioritization, data accuracy, ownership, collaborationData accuracy < 3
Engineering managerTechnical depth, people development, delivery, prioritization, communicationPeople development < 3
Customer successAccount ownership, communication, problem decomposition, empathy, data fluencyAccount ownership < 3

Funnel diagnostics (advanced)

Prompt 1 — Bottleneck mapping~5–10 min

You are a senior recruitment analyst. Map funnel metrics to bottleneck stage and recommend 3 interventions with owners.

Role: [job title]
Role family: [IC sales / HR / ops / eng mgr / CS]
Role location: [Remote – US, Hybrid – City, State, or On-site – City, State]
State: [two-letter, e.g., CA]
Target time-to-fill (days): [ ]

90-day funnel:
- Applicants: [ ]   Recruiter screens: [ ]   Hiring manager screens: [ ]
- Onsite/panel: [ ]   Offers: [ ]   Accepts: [ ]

Trend vs prior 90 days: [stable / declining / improving + 1 sentence]

Output a table: Stage | Signal | Action | Owner. Then 1 sentence on the single biggest leak.
Sample output — Account Executive, Remote – US
  • Stage: Recruiter screen → HM screen · Signal: 60% drop · Action: add 3-question discovery probe to recruiter screen · Owner: recruiter
  • Stage: Onsite → Offer · Signal: only 2 offers from 14 onsites (14%) · Action: calibrate panel on discovery rubric · Owner: HRBP
  • Stage: Offer → Accept · Signal: 50% acceptance · Action: open comp band conversation in screen, not at offer · Owner: recruiter
Biggest leak: onsite-to-offer — panel calibration is the highest-ROI fix this quarter.

Chapter 2Quality bar

Sourcing quality bar

AI-generated Boolean strings over-filter or miss synonyms. Verify in ATS and LinkedIn Recruiter before you run the campaign.

Outreach checklist

  • Role-specific (not "great opportunity") and location-specific (Remote – US / city)
  • No demographic proxies in screening criteria (see avoid-list below)
  • Comp band stated up front where state law permits or requires
  • Clear CTA and timeline (e.g., "10-min call this week")
  • Tracked in ATS with source and outreach_template tags
  • Response rate reviewed weekly; templates A/B tested monthly

Demographic proxies to avoid

"recent graduate"Age"0–3 years in the role"

Audit reminderRun a quarterly sourcing audit: response rate, interview rate, offer rate by channel. Flag any channel where one demographic group falls below 80% of the top group's pass-through (4/5ths rule heuristic).

Chapter 3Work sample

Work-sample prompts & rubric

Work samples test observable output. Use them when role output is visible (writing sample, case exercise, portfolio review, mock call).

Design rules

  • Same exercise for every finalist in the role
  • Rubric scored before seeing the candidate's name
  • Time-boxed (30–60 min) to reduce unpaid-labor burden
  • Pay if > 90 min (e.g., $150 flat) — strengthens trust and inclusion
  • Document accommodations in advance (extra time, alternative format)

Work-sample rubric (4 anchors)

RatingWhat it looks like
4 ExceptionalReframes the problem, names trade-offs, ships a complete artifact with self-critique
3 StrongSolves the problem as stated, complete artifact, minor self-critique
2 DevelopingPartial solution; missing one critical dimension; needs prompting
1 Below barMisreads the prompt; incomplete artifact; no self-critique
FairnessAccommodate disabilities; document alternatives in advance. A candidate requesting extra time is the same signal as one who doesn't — score the artifact, not the timing.

Chapter 4Rubric

Rubric: Communication (1–4)

One competency, four behaviorally anchored levels. Print this page; interviewers score with cited evidence, not "felt like a 3".

4 ExceptionalProactive updates without prompting; frames trade-offs for stakeholders; checks understanding with non-experts; comfortable in writing and live.
3 StrongClear updates when asked; occasional reactive communication; readable written work; structured live answers.
2 DevelopingVague status updates; defends position without business framing; written work needs editing; meanders in live answers.
1 Below barSurprises stakeholders; poor written documentation; misreads room in live setting.

Probes if scoring is ambiguous

  • "What did you write down before that meeting?"
  • "Who didn't know, and when did they find out?"
  • "Walk me through the slide you'd send to the CEO on this."

Build similar tables for each competency on your scorecard. Reuse anchors across role families where the competency is shared.

Rubric: Ownership & Collaboration

Ownership (1–4)

4 ExceptionalAnticipates blockers; delivers without reminders; pulls scope toward the goal, not headcount.
3 StrongDelivers on commitments with light guidance; raises risks before they bite.
2 DevelopingNeeds frequent direction; partial delivery; raises risks after they bite.

Collaboration (1–4)

4 ExceptionalBuilds working contracts with peers; navigates disagreement without damaging relationships; lifts the team's output, not just theirs.

Chapter 5Calibration

90-minute calibration drill

Run before every new role family. Goal: every interviewer scores the same answer the same way using the rubric anchors on pp. 13–14.

Setup (10 min)

  • Facilitator: HRBP. Participants: the full interview panel (3–5 people).
  • Each interviewer prints the rubric for their assigned competency.
  • Pick two volunteers from the panel: one plays the candidate, one plays the interviewer. The rest score independently.

Part 1 — Why these competencies (20 min)

Facilitator walks the panel through the competency model (Prompt 2 output) and the role family's known patterns. Each interviewer states their competency in one sentence and what a 4 looks like.

Part 2 — Internal mock interview (40 min)

The volunteer interviewer asks two STAR questions from the bank (p. 11). The volunteer candidate answers with a pre-agreed mix: one strong, one weak. Every other panel member scores independently on paper, no talking.

Facilitator script"You'll see a strong and a weak answer. Score each independently. If you can't score with evidence, write the probe you'd ask. We compare scores at the end — splits are the point of this exercise."

Part 3 — Compare & commit (30 min)

Reveal scores. Walk through every split of 2+ points. For each split: the higher scorer cites evidence, the lower scorer cites evidence, the panel agrees on the rubric anchor that fits. Update the rubric only if the panel agrees the anchor is wrong, not the scoring.

Output of the drill: a shared understanding of anchors; documented examples for the prompt library; a calibrated panel ready to interview real candidates the next day.

Sample debrief transcript

Account Executive role · Candidate "C" · 3-person panel. Annotations on the right show what the protocol (p.18) is doing in each move. Lightly edited from a real debrief; names anonymized.

Recruiter
"Scores are in. Lowest-scoring competency is Pipeline discipline at 2 average. We start there. No 'overall' until every competency is reviewed."
Move: opens with the lowest score — protocol step 2. No "I loved them" anchoring.
Sales lead
"I gave a 2. Evidence: when I asked about their last 5 deals by stage, they described them by feel — 'this one's hot, this one's lukewarm.' No stage criteria, no forecast accuracy named."
HM
"I had a 3. My evidence: they named MEDDIC by name in the work sample and walked the stages cleanly. So we're a 2 vs 3 split."
Move: 2 vs 3 split surfaced with cited evidence — protocol step 3.
Recruiter
"Two questions for the split. One: which moment carries more weight for the role — the live answer or the work sample? Two: what's the knockout we set on this?"
HRBP
"Knockout was Pipeline 3+. So if the panel calls it a 2, we don't proceed — regardless of other strengths."
Move: knockout invoked before the panel can argue around it (p.4).
Sales lead
"The live answer carries more weight. Work sample was tight because the prompt told them the stages. Live, they had to produce them and they didn't."
HM
"Hearing that, I'd revise to a 2. We have the evidence problem, not a rubric problem."
Recruiter
"Decision call. HM, your call — tied to the competency scores."
HM
"No hire. Strong elsewhere, but knockout fails. We'll send the decline today, ask the recruiter to test the pipeline probe earlier in screen."

Chapter 5Protocol

Debrief protocol

One page. Print it. The transcript on p.17 is this protocol in action.

  1. Scores submitted before the meeting — no exceptions. Late scores are not part of the debrief.
  2. Review competency-by-competency, not "overall gut feel" first. Start with the lowest-average competency.
  3. Discuss splits of 2+ points with cited evidence. Higher scorer first, then lower scorer, then rubric anchor agreement.
  4. Hiring manager states the decision tied to the competency scores. Recruiter writes the one-line rationale into the ATS.
Anti-anchoring rulesNo "I loved them" before evidence review. No "I just had a feeling" without a probe. No reopening a knockout failure by averaging.

Closing the loop

  • If no-hire: send the decline within 48 hours; capture one process learning.
  • If hire: open the offer talk track (p.19) within 24 hours.
  • Quarterly: review score distributions by interviewer; flag any >1.5 point divergence from panel mean.

Chapter 6Negotiation

Offer negotiation plays

Standing rules

  • Pre-approved bands before the call — recruiter can move within band without re-asking
  • Separate level / comp / start date / equity — negotiate one at a time
  • Document every verbal promise in writing within 24 hours
  • One internal approver per role; no surprises from a second exec

4 counter-offer scenarios

ScenarioPlayWhat not to say
Competing offer at higher baseConfirm the competing offer is real and written; match up to band ceiling; never exceed band without re-approval. If band ceiling is below the competing offer, decline cleanly and capture the band-vs-market data."We can probably do that."
Level bump askAnchor on the scorecard. A level bump = the candidate scored at that level in panel. If not, decline and offer a 6-month review path."We'll bump the title."
Start-date push (3+ weeks)Confirm hiring manager's coverage plan; offer to backfill the first week with contract; close with a written start date."Whenever works."
Equity askRefresh on the band; offer trade-off (base vs equity) within band ceiling; never improvise — escalate if the ask is above band."Sure, I'll get it approved."
Not legal advice. Have offers, equity grants, and accelerated comp reviewed by counsel per your state. AI drafts are starting points only.

  • Evidence-based scorecard notes only — no "felt off"
  • Quarterly score-distribution review by interviewer; flag >1.5 point divergence
  • Culture assessed as values behaviors, not social likeness
  • Adverse-impact review at the role-family level annually with counsel

RetentionExit interview

Retention & exit-interview playbook

Run the exit interview live or async; theme analysis happens after the person is gone, with anonymized notes.

5-question exit-interview script

  1. "What was the actual reason you started looking — not the polite answer?"
  2. "Which of: comp / role / manager / growth / process — best describes the trigger? Why?"
  3. "Tell me about a specific moment you decided this wasn't right."
  4. "What would have kept you 12 more months — be specific?"
  5. "What should we change for the next person in this seat?"

You are an attrition analyst. Group themes from these anonymized exit-interview notes into 5 buckets: comp, role design, manager, growth, process. Output a table and a one-sentence headline of the dominant theme.

Subject 1Structured hiring rolling out for [role family] — what changes Monday

Team, starting Monday we're piloting a structured hiring loop for [role family]. Three things change:

1. Each interviewer owns one competency, scored 1–4 with cited evidence. Rubrics attached.
2. Scores submitted in writing before the debrief — no exceptions.
3. The debrief opens with the lowest-scoring competency, not "overall thoughts".

Why: our last 5 loops averaged 14% onsite-to-offer. We have a calibration problem, not a candidate problem.

Action: read the attached rubrics (10 min) before Friday's calibration drill (90 min, on calendar).
Subject 2Calibration drill Friday — what to bring

Friday 10:00–11:30. Bring your printed rubric and one example from a past interview where you'd score differently if you had this rubric.

Format: 10 min setup, 20 min why-these-competencies, 40 min internal mock interview (volunteer pair), 30 min compare-and-commit.

HR team rollout — 4-week pilot

Pick one role family. Do not roll out to the whole team on day one. Measure adoption before you expand.

Week 1

Publish & align

  • Pick the role family pilot (one role, two open seats max)
  • Publish scorecard + rubrics (pp.13–15)
  • Send Email 1 (p.27) to the panel
  • Schedule the calibration drill
KPI: 100% of panel has the rubric printed by Friday.
Week 2

Calibrate the panel

  • 90-min calibration drill (p.16)
  • Document anchor disagreements in the prompt library
  • Send Email 2 (p.27)
KPI: no splits >2 points after the drill on the practice candidate.
Week 3

Run 1–3 hires

  • Live interviews; scores submitted in writing before debrief
  • Debrief protocol enforced (transcript pattern on p.17)
  • Decline within 48h; offer within 24h
KPI: 100% of debriefs open with the lowest competency, not "overall".
Week 4

Retro & expand

  • 15-min hire-#1 retro (Email 3 on p.27)
  • One process learning written into prompt library (p.30)
  • Decide: expand to next role family or iterate this one
KPI: 1 documented learning + a go/iterate decision in writing.
