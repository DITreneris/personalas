Chapter 1Core skill

The HR Prompt Formula

Weak prompts sound like texting a colleague: vague and context-free. Strong prompts give the model a role, one task, hiring context, output shape, and guardrails.

FieldWhat to include
RoleWho the AI acts as (e.g., recruitment analyst, interview specialist)
TaskOne deliverable (diagnose funnel, draft interview plan, phrase offer)
ContextRole title, location (US), stage, company size — no candidate PII
FormatBullets, table, max length, tone
ConstraintsEEO-aware language, US formats ($, MM/DD/YYYY), what to avoid
Weak

Give me interview questions for a sales manager.

Strong

Role: interview specialist. Task: 5 behavioral + 3 situational questions for a remote US Account Executive, mid-level, competency: discovery. Format: table with follow-up probes.

Pro tip: Reuse the same five-field skeleton for every prompt in this guide — only the Role and Task change.

Chapter 2Prompt 1

Diagnose where hiring is stuck

Prompt 1 — Where are we stuck?~5–10 min

You are a recruitment analyst. Your goal is to identify where recruitment is stuck and what to change, using the numbers.

Help me understand our recruitment challenges.

Company/location: [company, e.g., New York, NY]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – New York, NY, or On-site – Austin, TX]
Street Address: [optional, e.g., 123 Market St]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]
Zip Code: [optional, e.g., 10001]

We have:
- Number of candidates: [ ]
- Number of interviews: [ ]
- Number of offers: [ ]
- How many accepted: [ ]

Explain simply:
1. Where are we most stuck?
2. Why might that be?
3. What can we change this week?
Sample AI output — anonymized AE role, Austin, TX

1. Most stuck: interview-to-offer (only 2 offers from 14 interviews = 14%).

2. Likely cause: screening passes candidates who miss the discovery competency you score in panel; rubric drift between interviewers.

3. This week: tighten the screen with a 3-question discovery probe; calibrate panel on one shared rubric anchor.

Why this is good: cites your own numbers, names one root cause, gives one action you can run this week.

Chapter 2Prompt 2

Define who really fits

Prompt 2 — Ideal profile~5–10 min

You are a candidate profile specialist. Your goal is to clearly describe the ideal candidate for the role (traits, what can be taught, motivation).

Help me clearly describe who would fit us best.

Company/location: [company, e.g., New York, NY]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – New York, NY, or On-site – Austin, TX]
Street Address: [optional, e.g., 123 Market St]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]
Zip Code: [optional, e.g., 10001]
Best performer in this role: [what are they like?]
Why people left before: [ ]

Answer simply:
1. What 5 traits must they have?
2. What can be taught?
3. Why would this person consider changing jobs?
4. What would they fear most?
Sample profile — HR Generalist, hybrid NY

Must-have traits: stakeholder communication, judgment under ambiguity, process discipline, written clarity, follow-through on commitments.

Teachable: ATS specifics, comp band research, our policy library.

Motivation to switch: wants more scope than a recruiting-only role; values being trusted with manager coaching.

Top fear: being stuck in admin work without strategy exposure.

Why this is good: traits are job-related and observable, not "team player" filler.

Chapter 3Prompt 3

Rewrite a plain-language job ad

Prompt 3 — Job ad rewrite~3–5 min

You are a job ad editor. Rewrite this job ad so the candidate feels it's written for them.

Company/location: [company, e.g., San Francisco, CA]
Role location: [Remote – US / Hybrid + City, State / On-site + City, State]
Street Address: [optional, e.g., 123 Market St]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., CA]
Zip Code: [optional, e.g., 94105]
Text: [paste current job ad here]
Compensation: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]

Do: clear opening, plain language, concrete examples, clear apply CTA, US-friendly formatting, EEO-aware language.
Before

We are seeking a results-driven, dynamic HR professional with a passion for excellence to join our fast-paced team and drive strategic initiatives across the business.

After

Help our 60-person team hire 20 people this year in San Francisco, CA. You'll partner with two hiring managers, run intake meetings, and own the candidate experience end to end. Base: $85,000–$105,000.

US tipUse $ with commas, two-letter state codes, and Remote – US where applicable. Verify pay transparency rules for your state.

Chapter 3Prompt 4

Find more candidates today

Prompt 4 — Sourcing~3–5 min

You are a candidate sourcing consultant. Give 3 simple ways to find more candidates today.

Company/location: [company, e.g., Austin, TX]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – Austin, TX, or On-site – Austin, TX]
Street Address: [optional, e.g., 123 Market St]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., TX]
Zip Code: [optional, e.g., 73301]

Suggest:
- What to write on LinkedIn (1 post, 3 sentences max)
- How to ask your network (1 message, no PII)
- How to message a passive candidate (no PII, no demographic proxies)
Sample output — Account Executive, Remote – US

LinkedIn post: "We're hiring a Remote – US AE with $85k–$105k base + uncapped commission. If you've sold to mid-market HR tech buyers, I'd love to compare notes. Apply or DM — no résumé required for the first call."

Network ask: "Who's the best mid-market AE you've worked with in the last 2 years (not on your team today)? I'm hiring; happy to share comp band and process before any intros."

Passive outreach: Reference one specific public win (talk, post, case study), state comp band openly, end with "10-minute call this week?"

Why this is good: each channel has a concrete action, transparent comp, no demographic proxies.

Chapter 4Prompt 5

Structured interview plan

Prompt 5 — Better interview~5–10 min

You are an interview specialist. Create a simple structured interview plan for this role.

Company/location: [company, e.g., Chicago, IL]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – Chicago, IL, or On-site – Chicago, IL]
Street Address: [optional, e.g., 123 Market St]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., IL]
Zip Code: [optional, e.g., 60601]

Give:
- 5 behavioral questions (STAR format)
- 3 situational questions
- How to tell if they're really a fit
- What to watch out for

Format as a table: Question | What to listen for | Follow-up probe

Filled interview kit — sample (HR Generalist, Hybrid Chicago, IL)

QuestionListen forProbe
Tell me about a manager you coached through a hard hiring decision.Specific actions; named trade-offWhat did the manager change because of you?
Describe a time you said no to a hiring manager.Owned position; data citedHow did the relationship recover?
Walk me through a recent process you simplified.Before / after metricWhat broke when you simplified it?
You have 12 open roles and 2 recruiters. How do you triage Monday?Prioritization frameworkWhat gets cut, and how do you tell stakeholders?

Chapter 5Prompts 6–7

Declines & offers

Prompt 6 — Why candidates decline~3–5 min

You are a decline-pattern analyst. Look at why candidates are turning us down and what we can change.

Company/location: [company, e.g., New York, NY]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – New York, NY, or On-site – New York, NY]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]
Salary range: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]
What we offer: [ ]
Recent decline reasons we heard: [ ]

Give: 3 likely root causes; how to talk with the next candidate; one process change we could make.

Prompt 7 — Present the offer~3–5 min

You are an offer-call specialist. Help me phrase a job offer so the person feels the value.

Role: [job title]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – City, State, or On-site – City, State]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]
Salary range: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]
What we offer: [ ]
Start date target: [MM/DD/YYYY]

Do: short clear text; lead with the main benefit; friendly professional tone; US comp format.
Sample offer talk track — AE, Remote – US

"I'm calling with the offer. Base $95,000, OTE $170,000, full medical, 4 weeks PTO. We'd like you to start 06/02/2026. The reason we're excited: your discovery work in the panel was the strongest we've seen. What questions can I answer before you talk to your family?"

Why this is good: leads with the number, names the reason they got the offer, ends with an open question — not pressure.
Not legal advice. Have comp and offer letters reviewed per your state and counsel. AI output is a draft only.

PDF-onlyPrompt 8

Candidate experience audit

Not in the free 10-prompt builder. Use after 5+ interviews close (hire or no-hire) to find friction before it costs you the next finalist.

Prompt 8 — Audit the last 5 interviews~5–10 min

You are a candidate experience analyst. Audit our last 5 interview loops for friction and drop-off risk. No candidate PII below — counts and patterns only.

Role: [job title]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – City, State, or On-site – City, State]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]
Loops audited (count): [ ]
Days from application to offer (range): [e.g., 14–32]
Rescheduled interviews (count): [ ]
Take-home assignments used (Y/N): [ ]
Decline reasons heard (anonymized): [ ]

Output a table: Theme | Evidence | Fix this week | Owner. Then 1 sentence: the single biggest risk to our next finalist.
Sample audit — AE role, 5 loops, Remote – US
  • Theme — Scheduling drag: avg 24 days application to offer · evidence: 3 of 5 reschedules · fix: hold panel slots 2 weeks ahead · owner: recruiter.
  • Theme — Take-home fatigue: 90-min unpaid case · evidence: 2 candidates withdrew at this stage · fix: cut to 45 min or pay $150 · owner: hiring manager.
  • Theme — Silent gaps: 5+ business days between rounds · evidence: 2 of 5 loops · fix: same-day debrief, 48h decision SLA · owner: HRBP.
Biggest risk to the next finalist: the unpaid 90-minute case combined with a 5-day silence gap.

Not in the free 10-prompt builder. Use after every panel to keep the manager focused on evidence, not vibes.

Prompt 9 — Structured debrief~5 min prep

You are a debrief facilitator. Generate a structured 20-minute debrief script for the hiring manager and recruiter, anchored on competency evidence (no "gut feel" first).

Role: [job title]
Role location: [e.g., Remote – US, Hybrid – City, State, or On-site – City, State]
Competencies scored: [list]
Score scale: 1–4
Panel size: [number of interviewers]

Output:
1. Opening line (recruiter)
2. Round-robin order (lowest-scoring competency first)
3. 3 questions the recruiter must ask if scores split 2 vs 4
4. Decision phrasing options: Strong hire / Hire / No hire / Strong no hire

Sample filled scorecard — AE candidate "C", panel of 3

CompetencyRatingEvidence (required)
Discovery3Asked 4 of 5 expected probes; missed budget timing.
Objection handling4Reframed two stalls with specific customer references.
Pipeline discipline2Talked through deals by feel, not stage criteria.
Communication3Clear, structured; one over-long answer (8 min).

Recommendation: Hire — strengths in handling, gap in pipeline rigor coachable in 90 days with manager weekly review.

SafetyVerify

AI safety for HR

7-point verification (before you act on output)

  • Accurate — facts, comp ranges, and requirements checked against source data
  • Aligned — matches the role profile from Prompt 2
  • EEO-aware — no biased language, no "culture fit" proxies
  • No PII — no candidate identifiers in the prompt, the chat history, or screenshots
  • Policy-compliant — your organization's AI and data rules followed
  • Manager-approved — hiring manager signed off on JD and questions
  • Not a final legal doc — offers, policies, comp letters need human review
Never use AI alone forFinal hire / no-hire decisions, adverse impact analysis, or individualized compensation without approval.

ReferenceGlossary

Glossary, mistakes & sources

TermMeaning
Structured interviewSame questions and criteria for every candidate in the role
STARSituation, Task, Action, Result — behavioral answer format
KnockoutMust-have requirement; missing it = no hire regardless of other scores
FunnelApplicants → screens → interviews → offers → accepts

Six mistakes to avoid

  1. Skipping Prompt 2 and writing the ad first
  2. Vague prompts with no role, location, or stage
  3. Pasting résumés into public AI tools
  4. Accepting the first AI draft without verification
  5. Different interviewers asking different questions
  6. Using AI for final hiring decisions without documentation

5 takeaways

  1. Profile before job ad. Run Prompt 2 before you touch the JD.
  2. Structured beats improvised for fairness, quality, and defensibility.
  3. Evidence before "gut feel" in every debrief (use Prompt 9).
  4. US formats matter — $, two-letter states, MM/DD/YYYY, Remote – US.
