Chapter 1Core skill

The HR Prompt Formula

Weak prompts sound like texting a colleague: vague and context-free. Strong prompts give the model a role, one task, hiring context, output shape, and guardrails.

FieldWhat to include
RoleWho the AI acts as (e.g., recruitment analyst, interview specialist)
TaskOne deliverable (diagnose funnel, draft interview plan, phrase offer)
ContextRole title, location (US), stage, company size — no candidate PII
FormatBullets, table, max length, tone
ConstraintsEEO-aware language, US formats ($, MM/DD/YYYY), what to avoid
Weak

Give me interview questions for a sales manager.

Strong

Role: interview specialist. Task: 5 behavioral + 3 situational questions for a remote US Account Executive, mid-level, competency: discovery. Format: table with follow-up probes.

Chapter 2Prompts 1–2

Diagnose the funnel & define the profile

Prompt 1 — Where are we stuck?~5–10 min

You are a recruitment analyst. Your goal is to identify where recruitment is stuck and what to change, using the numbers.

Company/location: [company, e.g., New York, NY]
Role: [job title]
State: [two-letter State, e.g., NY]

We have:
- Number of candidates: [ ]
- Number of interviews: [ ]
- Number of offers: [ ]
- How many accepted: [ ]

Explain simply:
1. Where are we most stuck?
2. Why might that be?
3. What can we change this week?

Prompt 2 — Who really fits us?~5–10 min

You are a candidate profile specialist. Your goal is to clearly describe the ideal candidate (traits, what can be taught, motivation).

Role: [job title]
Best performer in this role: [what are they like?]
Why people left before: [ ]

Answer simply:
1. What 5 traits must they have?
2. What can be taught?
3. Why would this person consider changing jobs?
4. What would they fear most?

Good output: Specific traits tied to the role — not generic “team player” lists.

Chapter 3Prompts 3–4

Job ad & sourcing

Prompt 3 — Plain-language job ad~3–5 min

You are a job ad editor. Rewrite this job ad so the candidate feels it's written for them.

Company/location: [company, e.g., San Francisco, CA]
Role location: [Remote – US / Hybrid / On-site + City, State]
Text: [paste here]
Compensation: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]

Do: clear opening, plain language, concrete examples, clear apply CTA, US-friendly formatting.

Prompt 4 — Find more people today~3–5 min

You are a candidate sourcing consultant. Give 3 simple ways to find more candidates today:

Role: [job title]
Company/location: [e.g., Austin, TX]

Suggest:
- What to write on LinkedIn
- How to ask your network
- How to message someone directly
US tipUse $ with commas, two-letter state codes, and “Remote – US” where applicable. Verify pay transparency rules for your state.

Chapter 4Prompt 5

Structured interview plan

Prompt 5 — Better interview~5–10 min

You are an interview specialist. Create a simple interview plan for this role:

Role: [job title]
Company/location: [e.g., Chicago, IL]

Give:
- 5 questions that help understand the person
- 3 situational questions
- How to tell if they're really a fit
- What to watch for

Format as a table: Question | What to listen for | Follow-up probe

Sample output shape

QuestionListen forProbe
Tell me about a time you…Specific actions, not “we”What was your personal contribution?
How would you handle…?Structured thinkingWhat would you do first on day one?

Chapter 5Prompts 6–7

Declines & offers

Prompt 6 — Why candidates decline

Role: [job title]
Salary range: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]
What we offer: [ ]

Give: 3 possible reasons; how to talk with the candidate; what we could improve.

Prompt 7 — Present the offer

Help me phrase a job offer so the person feels the value.

Role: [job title]
Salary range: [e.g., $85,000–$105,000]
What we offer: [ ]

Do: short clear text; highlight main benefit; friendly professional tone.
Not legal advice. Have comp and offer letters reviewed per your state and counsel. AI output is a draft only.

PracticeChecklist

15-minute hiring kickoff

Run this before you open ChatGPT. Align hiring manager and HR on one page.

  • Role title, level, and reporting line defined
  • 3 must-have competencies (not 15 nice-to-haves)
  • Salary band approved internally
  • Interview panel named (who owns which competency)
  • Target start date and funnel goal (applicants → offers)
  • Knockout criteria documented (e.g., must work US hours)
  • Where we are stuck today (use Prompt 1 numbers)

After kickoff

Run Prompt 2 → 3 → 4 in order. Do not skip the profile step — it prevents generic job ads.

SafetyVerify

AI safety for HR

7-point verification (before you use output)

  • Accurate — facts, comp ranges, and requirements checked
  • Aligned — matches the role profile you defined
  • EEO-aware — no biased language or “culture fit” proxies
  • No PII — no candidate identifiers in the prompt or saved chat
  • Policy-compliant — your org’s AI and data rules followed
  • Manager-approved — hiring manager signed off on JD and questions
  • Not a final legal doc — offers and policies need human review
Never use AI alone forFinal hire/no-hire decisions, adverse impact analysis, or individualized comp without approval.

ReferenceGlossary

Glossary & beginner mistakes

TermMeaning
Structured interviewSame questions and criteria for every candidate in the role
STARSituation, Task, Action, Result — behavioral answer format
KnockoutMust-have requirement; missing it = no hire regardless of other scores
FunnelApplicants → screens → interviews → offers → accepts

Six mistakes to avoid

  1. Skipping Prompt 2 and writing the ad first
  2. Vague prompts with no role or stage
  3. Pasting résumés into public AI tools
  4. Accepting the first AI draft without verification
  5. Different interviewers asking different questions
  6. Using AI for final hiring decisions without documentation

10 takeaways

  1. Structured beats improvised for fairness and quality.
  2. Profile before job ad.
  3. Tables beat paragraphs for interview plans.
  4. AI drafts; humans decide.
  5. Document criteria before interviews start.
  6. US formats matter for credibility.
  7. Decline analysis improves the next offer.
  8. Keep prompts in a shared HR library.
